# Active Imagination

**A Jungian technique of consciously engaging with unconscious figures and content through dialogue, visualization, or creative expression.**

## Overview
Active imagination is the primary practical method of Jungian individuation. Rather than passively receiving dreams or fantasies, the practitioner enters the imagery with intention — dialoguing with inner figures, continuing a dream, or giving form to an emotion through art or writing. It is distinct from passive daydreaming: the ego participates actively while the unconscious leads. Jung developed the technique during his own confrontation with the unconscious, documented in *The Red Book*.

## Key Aspects
- Bridges conscious and unconscious — the ego meets inner figures as autonomous presences
- Can be practiced through writing, drawing, painting, movement, or inner dialogue
- Not passive fantasy — requires focused attention and ego engagement
- Core practice in the individuation process
- Jung used it extensively in *The Red Book* (1913–1930)
