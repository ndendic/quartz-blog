# Collective Unconscious

**A shared psychic layer beneath individual consciousness, containing inherited archetypes common to all humanity.**

## Overview
The collective unconscious is Jung's most radical and influential idea. Unlike the personal unconscious (formed from individual experience), the collective unconscious is inherited — a deep psychic substrate shared across the species. It is the source of archetypes, which manifest in dreams, myths, and religious imagery worldwide regardless of cultural contact. Jung saw it as evidence that individual minds are rooted in something larger.

## Key Aspects
- Distinct from the personal unconscious (which holds repressed individual experiences)
- Contains archetypes — not personally acquired but inherited
- Manifests consistently in myths, dreams, and art across unconnected cultures
- Not directly accessible but inferred from repeating symbolic patterns
- Jung's parallel to Walter Russell's concept of the Universal Mind
