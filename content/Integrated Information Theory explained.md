## Connections

### Related vault notes

- [[Carl Jung]] — IIT's concept of "integrated information" as the substrate of consciousness parallels Jung's model of the psyche as a self-integrating system. Jung's individuation process — bringing unconscious contents into conscious relation — is essentially an increase in psychic integration, which IIT would predict correlates with higher phi.

- [[Synchronicity]] — IIT's discussion of group minds and hive minds (1:44:05) raises the question of whether collective consciousness could emerge from sufficiently integrated human groups. This is the territory Jung was mapping with synchronicity — meaningful correlations arising from a shared psychic substrate.

- [[Walter Russell]] — IIT's insistence on an intrinsic perspective (1:08:56) — that consciousness is not observable from the outside but only from within — echoes Russell's claim that the universe knows itself from within. Both reject the idea that third-person observation can capture the essence of experience.

- [[Jung and Technology]] — IIT's argument that LLMs and computers cannot be conscious because they lack intrinsic causal integration (21:46) is directly relevant to the blog draft. It provides a rigorous framework for distinguishing between intelligence and consciousness in technological systems.

- [[Convergence Map]] — IIT represents the "science of consciousness" approach: building a formal mathematical theory from phenomenological axioms. Its metaphysical implications (1:15:56) unexpectedly converge with idealist philosophy.

### Related clippings (Essentia Foundation series)

- [[Does Karl Friston's neuroscience point to non-dualism]] — Friston compares FEP and IIT at 1:13:15, framing them as complementary: IIT addresses what consciousness is (integrated information), while FEP addresses what consciousness does (minimize surprise). The two theories together form a more complete picture than either alone.

- [[Spacetime is the memory of a self knowing universe]] — Faggin's view that consciousness is fundamental and matter is its memory aligns with IIT's position that consciousness is intrinsic to certain physical structures. Both reject eliminativism and computationalism, though they disagree on whether consciousness requires physical substrate (IIT) or precedes it (Faggin).
