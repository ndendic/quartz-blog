## Connections

### Related vault notes

- [[Carl Jung]] — Faggin's concept of "Seity" — a conscious field that individuates from the One — is remarkably close to Jung's notion of the Self as the totality of the psyche, which individuates from the collective unconscious. Both describe a process where universal consciousness differentiates into individual nodes of awareness while remaining connected to the whole.

- [[Synchronicity]] — Faggin's claim that "there is no past" (37:29) and that spacetime is memory, not a container, reframes synchronicity from "coincidence in time" to "resonance in the memory structure of the universe." If all experience is recorded in the fabric of reality, meaningful connections between events become structural features, not anomalies.

- [[Walter Russell]] — This is perhaps the strongest connection in the vault. Russell described the universe as "God's thinking" made manifest through rhythmic interchange, with matter as the record of that thinking. Faggin's formulation — spacetime as "the permanent memory of the experience of the self knowing of One" — is almost a direct restatement of Russell's cosmology in the language of quantum physics.

- [[Jung and Technology]] — Faggin's argument that conscious AI is a fantasy (1:18:50) because consciousness is not computational but quantum-field-based adds a physicist's perspective to the blog draft's exploration of technology and psyche. His distinction between mind and spirit (33:57) could enrich the piece.

- [[Convergence Map]] — Faggin represents the convergence of semiconductor engineering, quantum physics, and mystical experience in a single person. His trajectory from inventing the CPU to developing a quantum theory of consciousness is itself a data point for the map.

### Related clippings (Essentia Foundation series)

- [[Does Karl Friston's neuroscience point to non-dualism]] — Both Friston and Faggin conclude that the self is not fundamental, but they arrive there from opposite directions. Friston sees the self as an inferential model (a story the brain tells); Faggin sees it as a temporary individuation of the One. The first is epistemological, the second ontological — yet they converge on non-dualism.

- [[Integrated Information Theory explained]] — IIT and Faggin's theory both treat consciousness as intrinsic rather than emergent, but disagree on mechanism. IIT grounds consciousness in causal integration of physical structures; Faggin grounds it in quantum fields that precede physical structure. Faggin's view is more radical — consciousness first, matter as its trace.
