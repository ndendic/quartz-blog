# Synchronicity

**Meaningful coincidences — events connected by meaning rather than causation.**

## Overview
Synchronicity is Jung's term for acausal connections between inner psychological events and outer circumstances. When an inner state (a dream, a thought, an emotion) coincides meaningfully with an outer event, and no causal link exists, Jung called this synchronistic. It is not superstition — Jung saw it as evidence of a deeper ordering principle connecting psyche and world, developed partly in dialogue with physicist Wolfgang Pauli.

## Key Aspects
- Coined by Jung; developed with physicist Wolfgang Pauli
- Acausal connecting principle — meaning, not mechanism, links events
- Often occurs during psychological transitions or heightened unconscious activity
- Challenges a purely mechanistic worldview without abandoning scientific rigor
- Has parallels in quantum physics discussions (non-locality, observer effect)
